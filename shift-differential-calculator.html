How Shift Differential Pay Works

Shift differential pay is additional compensation employers offer to workers who take on evening, overnight, weekend, or holiday shifts. Because these hours are less desirable than standard day shifts, employers add a premium to attract and retain employees willing to work them.

Two Types: Percentage vs. Flat Rate

Percentage differential: Your hourly rate increases by a set percentage during qualifying hours. A $20/hr base with a 15% evening differential becomes $23/hr. This approach scales with your base rate — senior workers with higher base pay earn more in absolute terms from the same percentage differential.

Flat-rate differential: A fixed dollar amount is added per hour (e.g., $2.00/hr extra). This is simpler to calculate and apply consistently across different pay grades.

FLSA Overtime and Shift Differentials

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, shift differential pay must be included in your "regular rate of pay" when calculating overtime. This means your overtime rate is 1.5× your effective blended rate — not 1.5× your base rate. If your effective rate with differential is $23/hr, your overtime rate is $34.50/hr, not the lower $30/hr (1.5× $20 base).

Tax Treatment

Shift differentials are taxed exactly like regular wages: federal and state income tax withholding, Social Security (6.2%), and Medicare (1.45%) all apply. There is no special lower tax rate for shift premiums.

FAQ

Shift differential questions answered

What is shift differential pay?
Shift differential pay is additional compensation for working evenings, nights, weekends, or holidays. It can be a percentage above your base rate (e.g., +15%) or a flat dollar amount per hour (e.g., +$2.00/hr). About 16% of full-time US workers receive some form of shift differential according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
How is shift differential calculated?
For percentage: effective rate = base rate × (1 + differential%). Example: $20/hr × 1.15 = $23/hr. For flat rate: effective rate = base rate + flat amount. Example: $20/hr + $2.50 = $22.50/hr. The premium only applies to hours actually worked at that shift type.
Is shift differential taxed differently?
No — shift differential pay is taxed exactly like regular wages. Federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and state income taxes all apply the same way. Your employer includes the differential in your gross pay and withholds taxes normally.
How does shift differential affect overtime?
Under FLSA, your overtime rate must be 1.5× your effective rate including the differential — not just 1.5× your base rate. So if your effective rate is $23/hr with differential, overtime must be at least $34.50/hr. Employers who pay overtime at 1.5× the base rate are underpaying and violating FLSA.
What is a typical shift differential percentage?
Evening shifts (3–11pm): 10–15% in most industries, up to 20% in healthcare. Night shifts (11pm–7am): 15–25%, up to 30% in hospitals. Weekend: 5–15%. Holiday: 50–100% (or time-and-a-half to double time). Rates vary by industry, employer, and collective bargaining agreements.
