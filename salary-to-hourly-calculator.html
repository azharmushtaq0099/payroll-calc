How to convert salary to hourly rate

The conversion formula is straightforward: divide your annual salary by the total hours you work in a year. For a standard 40-hour week across all 52 weeks, that's 2,080 hours — the most commonly used denominator. A $75,000 salary divided by 2,080 yields $36.06 per hour. Going the other direction, multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 to get the equivalent annual salary.

How many hours are in a work year?

The two figures you'll encounter most often are 2,080 hours (no vacation: 40 hrs × 52 weeks) and 2,000 hours (with 10 paid vacation days, the US median). Federal government pay tables use 2,087 hours; many corporate HR departments use 1,950 for workers who take full PTO. The difference matters: a $60,000 salary equals $28.85/hr over 2,080 hours but $30.77/hr over 1,950 — a $1.92 gap that compounds significantly when comparing job offers.

Teachers and academic-year employees — typically working 9–10 months — often base hourly calculations on 1,560–1,800 hours, which is why a teacher's annual salary can look inflated relative to a calendar-year employee's. Nurses frequently work three 12-hour shifts per week (36 hours), not 40, meaning a $90,000 nursing salary equals $48.08/hr at 1,872 annual hours — not $43.27 that the default 40-hr formula would produce. How overtime affects your effective hourly rate

If you regularly work overtime as a non-exempt employee, your effective annual earnings exceed what the simple hours × rate formula implies. Under the FLSA, overtime hours (over 40 per week) must be paid at 1.5× the regular rate. A worker earning $20/hr who regularly works 45 hours earns $20 × 40 + $30 × 5 = $950/week rather than $900 — a meaningful 5.6% premium that adds up over the year. Salaried exempt employees receive the same pay regardless of hours. If your employer regularly expects 50+ hours but your salary is set for 40, your true hourly rate is materially lower than the simple conversion shows.
