About Direct Deposit Forms

Where to Find Your Routing Number

  • Bottom-left corner of a personal check (first 9 digits)
  • Your bank's website or mobile app (Account Details section)
  • Call your bank directly — they can provide it instantly
  • Note: routing numbers on deposit slips may differ from checks

Where to Find Your Account Number

  • Bottom of a personal check (middle number, after routing)
  • Bank statement — listed prominently near the top
  • Online banking / mobile app — Account Details or Settings
  • Account numbers vary in length: typically 8–17 digits

What Happens After You Submit

Give the completed form to your HR or payroll department. Most employers process the change within 1–2 pay cycles. Some require a voided check to verify your banking information alongside the authorization form.

Frequently Asked Questions

A direct deposit authorization form is a document that authorizes your employer to electronically deposit your paycheck directly into your bank account. It includes your bank's routing number, your account number, the account type (checking or savings), and the amount or percentage of each paycheck to deposit. Once submitted to HR or payroll, your paychecks are deposited automatically on payday — no waiting for a paper check.
You need four key pieces of information: (1) your bank's 9-digit ABA routing number, (2) your account number, (3) the account type — checking or savings, and (4) whether to deposit your full paycheck or a specific dollar amount/percentage. Optionally, your employer may require your name, address, employee ID, and signature. A voided check is sometimes requested to verify the account information.
After submitting your direct deposit form to your employer's HR or payroll team, it typically takes 1–2 pay cycles (1–4 weeks) to activate. Some payroll systems process changes faster. Ask your payroll team about the cutoff date for changes to take effect on the next pay cycle.
