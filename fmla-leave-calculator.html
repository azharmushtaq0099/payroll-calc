FMLA Leave: What You Need to Know in 2026

The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) provides eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year. Understanding eligibility and planning the leave period reduces surprises on both sides.

The Two Eligibility Tests

  • 12-month tenure: Must have worked for the employer at least 12 months total (not necessarily consecutive). Breaks in service under 7 years count.
  • 1,250 hours: Must have actually worked 1,250 hours in the 12 months immediately before leave begins. Paid leave (PTO, vacation) generally does not count toward this total.

How Leave Hours Are Calculated

Leave entitlement is 12 workweeks — not 480 hours for everyone. If you work 30 hours per week, your 12 weeks = 360 hours. If you work 40 hours/week, 12 weeks = 480 hours. Hours scale to your actual scheduled work week.

Using PTO Concurrently

Employers may require (and employees may elect) to substitute accrued PTO, vacation, or sick leave for unpaid FMLA leave. This means you get paid during the PTO portion, but those days count against your 12-week FMLA bank — not in addition to it.

FAQ

FMLA leave questions answered

Who qualifies for FMLA?
You must work for a covered employer (50+ employees within 75 miles), have 12 months of tenure, and have logged 1,250 hours in the past year. Public agencies and schools have different thresholds.
Is FMLA leave paid?
FMLA itself is unpaid. However, employers may require you to use accrued PTO concurrently. Many states (CA, NY, WA, NJ, MA, CT, OR, CO) have paid family leave programs that provide wage replacement.
Can I take FMLA intermittently?
Yes. FMLA can be taken in any combination of continuous, reduced schedule, or intermittent blocks. All formats draw from the same 12-week bank. Your employer may ask for a medical certification and a schedule estimate.
Will my job be protected while on FMLA?
Yes. FMLA guarantees return to the same or an equivalent position with the same pay, benefits, and terms. Employers cannot retaliate for taking FMLA leave — termination while on FMLA is a serious legal violation.
