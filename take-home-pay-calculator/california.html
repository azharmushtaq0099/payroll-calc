About California Income Tax (2026)

California uses a 10-bracket progressive income tax system, with rates ranging from 1% on the first dollar of income to 13.3% on income over $1 million. For most wage earners, the effective marginal rate falls in the 6%–9.3% range. California does not conform to the federal standard deduction, meaning more of your income is taxable at the state level than at the federal level.

Key California Tax Facts

  • SDI (State Disability Insurance): California withholds 1.1% of your gross wages for SDI with no annual wage cap. On a $75,000 salary, that's an additional $825 per year — a deduction that does not appear in most generic calculators.
  • No federal standard deduction conformity: California's standard deduction is only $5,202 for single filers (2026) vs. the federal $15,000. This means your CA taxable income is significantly higher than your federal taxable income.
  • 13.3% — highest in the nation: California's top rate of 13.3% (12.3% + 1% Mental Health Services Tax on income over $1 million) is the highest individual income tax rate of any US state.

FAQ

California take-home pay questions answered

California's State Disability Insurance (SDI) is a mandatory payroll deduction of 1.1% of gross wages in 2026, with no wage cap. Unlike Social Security which stops at $176,100, California SDI applies to every dollar you earn. On a $100,000 salary, that's $1,100 per year withheld in addition to state income tax. SDI funds California's disability insurance and paid family leave programs. It does not appear in most generic national calculators, making CA take-home estimates from other tools often too high.
Three reasons. First, California's standard deduction for single filers is only $5,202 versus the federal $15,000 — so far more of your income is taxable at the state level. Second, California does not recognize many federal tax benefits (like student loan interest deductions). Third, once your income exceeds $66,295, California taxes the entire amount above that threshold at 9.3% — and that's before SDI. A California worker earning $80,000 pays more to the state than a New York worker at the same salary despite NY's higher headline rate, because NY's standard deduction is more generous.
Nine states have no individual income tax in 2026: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Residents of these states pay only federal income tax and FICA — no state withholding at all. Compare that to California (up to 13.3%) or New York (up to 10.9%) and the take-home difference on a $100,000 salary can exceed $8,000 per year.
The most effective strategies: (1) Maximize traditional 401(k) contributions — every dollar reduces your federal and state taxable income immediately. (2) Contribute to a Health FSA or HSA through payroll deduction — these reduce both income tax and FICA taxes, saving ~30 cents per dollar. (3) Review your W-4 withholding allowances — if you consistently receive a large tax refund, you are over-withholding and can adjust to keep more each paycheck. Use the pre-tax deductions section in this calculator to model the exact impact.
Gross pay is your total earnings for the pay period before any deductions — your full salary, hourly wages, or other compensation before the government or your employer takes anything out. Net pay (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state income tax, and any voluntary pre-tax deductions are subtracted. For example, a $60,000 annual salary is $2,308 bi-weekly gross — but take-home might be $1,650–$1,900 depending on your state, filing status, and deductions.
