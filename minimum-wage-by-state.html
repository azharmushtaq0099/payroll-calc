FAQ

Minimum wage questions answered

What is the federal minimum wage in 2026?
The federal minimum wage is $7.25 per hour in 2026, unchanged since July 24, 2009 — making it the longest period without a federal increase in US history. Employers must pay whichever is higher: the federal rate, state rate, or any applicable local (city/county) rate.
Which states have a $15 minimum wage in 2026?
As of 2026, states with minimum wages at or above $15/hr include: California ($16.50), Washington State ($16.66+), Connecticut ($16.35), New York ($16.00 state / $16.50 NYC), New Jersey ($15.49), Washington DC ($17.50), Illinois ($15.00), Massachusetts ($15.00), Maryland ($15.00), Delaware ($15.00), Rhode Island ($15.00), and Nebraska ($15.00 as of January 2026). Florida reaches $15.00 in September 2026.
Can a city have a higher minimum wage than the state?
Yes. Cities and counties can set minimum wages above the state rate. Examples: Seattle ($19.97 for large employers), San Francisco ($18.67), Denver ($18.81), Los Angeles ($17.28), Chicago ($16.20). The highest applicable rate — federal, state, or local — always governs. If a business operates in a city with a higher minimum wage than the state, it must pay the city rate to all employees working in that city.
What is the minimum wage for tipped workers?
The federal tipped minimum wage is $2.13/hr, provided tips bring total pay to at least $7.25/hr. However, many states do not allow this "tip credit" and require tipped workers to receive the full state minimum wage. States with no tip credit include California, Washington, Oregon, Montana, Nevada (since July 2024), Minnesota, and Alaska. Always check your state's specific rule — it varies significantly.
How often does the minimum wage increase?
Many states now index their minimum wage to inflation (CPI) and adjust automatically every January 1st. Examples include Washington, Colorado, Arizona, Oregon, and others. Some states have legislated scheduled increases (like Florida's Amendment 2, which increases the rate by $1 each September). The federal minimum wage requires an act of Congress and has not changed since 2009. Employers should check their state's rate each January.
