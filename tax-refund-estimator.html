How to Estimate Your Tax Refund (2026)

Your refund is simply the difference between what the IRS owes you and what you owe them:

Refund = Federal Tax Withheld − Tax Liability

If the number is positive, you get a refund. If negative, you owe that amount by April 15.

2026 Standard Deductions

  • Single: $15,000
  • Married Filing Jointly: $30,000
  • Head of Household: $22,500
  • Married Filing Separately: $15,000

Where to Find Your Withholding

Check your most recent pay stub for "Federal Income Tax YTD" or look at your W-2 Box 2. If you have multiple jobs, add all Box 2 amounts together.

FAQ

Tax refund questions answered

How accurate is this tax refund estimator?
This estimator uses official 2026 IRS tax brackets and standard deductions. It is accurate for W-2 employees with straightforward income. It does not account for tax credits (Child Tax Credit, EITC, etc.), capital gains, rental income, or AMT. For those, use the full income tax calculator or IRS Free File.
Why do I always get a big refund?
A big refund means too much was withheld from your paychecks all year. You gave the IRS an interest-free loan. To get more money in each paycheck instead, update your W-4 with your employer and claim more allowances or reduce extra withholding.
Can I still get a refund if I owe taxes?
Yes, if you are eligible for refundable tax credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) or Child Tax Credit, your refund can exceed your withholding. This estimator only covers the basic withholding vs. liability comparison and does not include credits.
When is the 2026 tax deadline?
The 2026 federal tax filing deadline for 2025 tax year returns is April 15, 2026. You can file for an automatic 6-month extension to October 15, 2026 — but any taxes owed must still be paid by April 15 to avoid interest and penalties.
