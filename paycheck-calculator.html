Quick Answer

Your net paycheck equals gross pay minus federal income tax (10%–37% brackets), Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and state income tax. A $1,000/week gross paycheck in Texas nets approximately $793–$812; the same paycheck in New York nets approximately $695–$725.

How your 2026 paycheck is calculated

Every US paycheck follows the same withholding sequence regardless of whether you are paid hourly or on salary. Understanding each step helps you budget correctly and verify your pay stub.

Federal income tax is withheld using the annualized percentage method from IRS Publication 15-T. Your per-period gross wages are multiplied by the number of pay periods per year to arrive at an annualized figure. The 2026 standard deduction ($15,000 single, $30,000 married) is subtracted, then marginal bracket rates from 10% to 37% are applied. The annual tax is divided back to a per-paycheck withholding amount.

FICA taxes — Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) — apply to gross wages before most deductions. Social Security stops once your wages hit the $176,100 annual wage base. Medicare has no cap; earners above $200,000 pay an additional 0.9% surcharge.

FAQ

Paycheck Calculator questions answered

Step 1: Calculate gross pay (hourly rate × hours, or salary ÷ pay periods). Step 2: Subtract any pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance). Step 3: Apply federal income tax using IRS Publication 15-T percentage method based on your filing status and annualized income. Step 4: Withhold Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) from gross wages. Step 5: Withhold state income tax per your state's tables. The remainder is net take-home pay.
Gross pay is your total earnings before any deductions — your hourly rate times hours worked, or your salary divided by pay periods. Net pay is what you actually receive after federal income tax, FICA taxes (Social Security + Medicare), state income tax, and any voluntary deductions are subtracted. For most US workers, net pay is 65–80% of gross pay depending on state and filing status.
For hourly workers, varying hours change gross pay and therefore tax withholding. For salaried workers, changes can occur when: the Social Security wage base is hit ($176,100 in 2026) and SS withholding stops, a bonus is paid at the 22% supplemental rate, W-4 withholding is adjusted, or benefit deductions change during open enrollment.
Three proven strategies: (1) Maximize traditional 401(k) contributions — each pre-tax dollar reduces your federal and state taxable income immediately. At a 22% bracket, $500/period in 401(k) costs you only $390 in take-home. (2) Enroll in an HSA or FSA — these reduce both income tax and FICA. (3) Adjust your W-4 — if you consistently get a large tax refund, you're over-withholding; increase allowances to keep more each paycheck.
