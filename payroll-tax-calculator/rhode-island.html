Rhode Island Tax Guide

Rhode Island Payroll Tax Overview 2026

Rhode Island uses a progressive income tax with a top rate of 5.99%. Higher earners pay a larger percentage of their income — different brackets apply to different income ranges.

Rhode Island has three income tax brackets with a top rate of 5.99% and mandatory TDI contributions from employees.

Rhode Island TDI provides up to 85% of average weekly wages for workers who are temporarily disabled and unable to work.

FAQ

Rhode Island payroll tax questions

What is the top Rhode Island income tax rate for 2026?
Rhode Island's top income tax rate is 5.99% for 2026. This is a marginal rate — it only applies to income above the top bracket threshold. Lower income falls into lower brackets. Use the calculator above to see your effective rate.
What taxes do Rhode Island employees pay on their paycheck?
Rhode Island employees pay: (1) Federal income tax (based on W-4 withholding); (2) Social Security tax (6.2% on wages up to $176,100); (3) Medicare tax (1.45%, plus 0.9% on income over $200,000); (4) Rhode Island state income tax (Up to 5.99% (progressive)); (5) TDI (Temporary Disability Insurance). The employer matches the Social Security and Medicare portions.
What payroll withholding form do Rhode Island employees use?
Rhode Island employees complete Rhode Island RI W-4 for state withholding in addition to the federal W-4. Employers use both forms to calculate the correct combined withholding amount.
