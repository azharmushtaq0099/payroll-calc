FAQ

Common payroll questions

Our calculators use the official IRS Publication 15 (Circular E) withholding tables and 2025/2026 tax brackets. Federal estimates are within $0.01 of IRS-standard payroll software. State taxes use the most current rates from each state's Department of Revenue. Results are for estimation — always verify with your payroll provider for final deductions.
The Social Security Administration adjusts the taxable wage base annually. For 2025 it was $176,100. The 2026 figure will be announced by SSA in October 2025 — our calculator is updated immediately when the official rate is released. Social Security tax is 6.2% for employees (and 6.2% matched by employers) up to that wage limit. Medicare (1.45%) has no wage cap.
Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), salaried employees earning below the exempt salary threshold ($684/week as of the 2024 rule, currently under legal review) must receive overtime pay. Salaried employees above the threshold in exempt roles (executive, administrative, professional) are not owed FLSA overtime. See our Overtime Pay Calculator for a detailed breakdown.
FUTA (Federal Unemployment Tax Act) is a federal employer-only tax of 6% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages — most employers pay a net 0.6% after the state credit. SUTA (State Unemployment Tax Act) varies by state, ranging from 0.1% to over 8% depending on your state and experience rating. See our Employer Payroll Tax Calculator for combined cost.
PTO can accrue three ways: (1) per pay period — annual days ÷ number of pay periods (e.g., 10 days ÷ 26 biweekly periods = 0.385 days/period); (2) per hour worked — a rate like 0.04 hours of PTO per hour worked = 1 day per 25 hours; (3) lump sum — full balance granted at the start of the year. Use our PTO Accrual Calculator to project balances under all three methods.
