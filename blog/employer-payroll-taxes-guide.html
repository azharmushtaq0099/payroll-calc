Employer Payroll Taxes in 2026: Every Tax You Need to Know

When you hire an employee, your actual cost is significantly more than their gross salary. Beyond wages, employers must pay federal and state payroll taxes that add 10–20% to payroll costs. Understanding these taxes is essential for accurate budgeting and compliance.

By FreePayrollCalc Editorial • Updated September 2026 • 9 min read

Federal taxes employers pay on wages

FICA matching — employer's 7.65%

Employers pay the same FICA amount that employees pay — 6.2% Social Security (on first $176,100 in 2026) + 1.45% Medicare on all wages. Unlike employees, employers do NOT pay the additional 0.9% Medicare surcharge for high earners.

FUTA — Federal Unemployment Tax

FUTA rate is 6% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages. Employers who pay their state unemployment taxes (SUTA) on time receive a 5.4% credit, making the effective federal rate 0.6%. Maximum annual FUTA per employee: $7,000 × 0.6% = $42.

FUTA is reported annually on Form 940. Deposits are required quarterly if accumulated liability exceeds $500.

State taxes employers pay

SUTA — State Unemployment Insurance

All states require employers to pay state unemployment insurance. Rates vary from 0.1% to 14% depending on:

  • The state's unemployment fund balance
  • Your company's "experience rating" — lower unemployment claims = lower rate
  • Industry (construction typically pays higher rates)
  • New employer rates: typically 2.7% (varies widely by state)

SUTA taxable wage bases range from $7,000 (same as FUTA in some states) to $62,000+ in states like Washington, Oregon, and North Dakota.

Workers' compensation insurance

Workers' comp is technically insurance, not a tax, but it's a mandatory employer cost in 49 states (Texas is optional). Rates are calculated per $100 of payroll and vary dramatically by industry and state:

  • Clerical workers: $0.20–$0.50 per $100 payroll
  • Retail workers: $0.80–$2.00 per $100
  • Construction: $5.00–$20.00 per $100

Total employer cost breakdown

Example: $50,000/year employee in Texas (office worker)
Gross wages: $50,000
FICA matching: $3,825 (7.65%)
FUTA: $42 (0.6% on $7,000)
SUTA (est. 2.7% on $9,000 base): $243
Workers' comp (est. $0.35 per $100): $175
Total employer taxes: $4,285 (~8.6% on top of wages)
Benefits not included — add $8,000–$15,000 for health insurance + 401k match

Frequently Asked Questions

FUTA (federal unemployment tax), SUTA (state unemployment insurance), and the employer's matching 7.65% FICA contribution are all paid entirely by the employer — never deducted from employee wages. Workers' compensation insurance is also an employer cost. The only employer-paid tax that employees also pay is FICA (they each pay their half).
Federal payroll taxes (withheld income tax + both employee and employer FICA) must be deposited based on your lookback period liability. "Monthly" depositors deposit by the 15th of the following month. "Semi-weekly" depositors must deposit within 3 business days of paydays falling Wednesday-Friday, and within 5 business days for Monday-Tuesday paydays. New employers start as monthly depositors.
