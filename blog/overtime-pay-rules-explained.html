Overtime Pay Rules Explained: FLSA, State Laws & Exemptions

Overtime pay rules govern when and how much extra pay non-exempt employees are entitled to for working more than standard hours. Understanding these rules protects both employees from wage theft and employers from costly DOL violations.

By FreePayrollCalc Editorial • Updated September 2026 • 10 min read

Federal overtime under the FLSA

The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) requires employers to pay non-exempt employees at least 1.5× their regular rate of pay for all hours worked over 40 in a single workweek. Key points:

  • The workweek is any fixed, regularly recurring 7-day period — not necessarily calendar week
  • Hours cannot be averaged across two weeks — 50 hours one week and 30 the next = 10 hours of overtime (the 50-hour week), even though the 2-week average is 40
  • Overtime applies to hours actually worked — paid time off, holidays, and sick days don't count toward the 40-hour threshold

Regular rate of pay: the real base for overtime

The overtime rate is 1.5× the "regular rate of pay" — which is not simply the base hourly rate. The regular rate must include:

  • Non-discretionary bonuses (bonuses employees are promised in advance)
  • Shift differentials
  • Production bonuses
  • Most other remuneration for employment

It does NOT include: discretionary bonuses decided at employer's sole discretion, gifts, vacation/sick pay, overtime premium pay itself, or reimbursements.

Example: Employee earns $18/hr + $200 non-discretionary bonus in a 50-hour week.
Regular rate = ($18 × 50 + $200) ÷ 50 = $22/hr
Overtime premium = $22 × 0.5 × 10 OT hours = $110
Total compensation = $900 wages + $200 bonus + $110 OT premium = $1,210

Who is exempt from overtime?

Certain employee classifications are exempt from FLSA overtime requirements:

  • Executive exemption: Primary duty is managing the enterprise or a department; regularly directs 2+ employees; authority to hire/fire (or recommendations carry significant weight); paid ≥ $684/week salary ($35,568/yr)
  • Administrative exemption: Primary duty is office/non-manual work related to management or general business operations; exercises discretion and independent judgment on significant matters; ≥ $684/week salary
  • Professional exemption: Primary duty requires advanced knowledge in a field of science or learning, acquired by prolonged course of specialized instruction; ≥ $684/week salary
  • Highly compensated employees: Total annual compensation ≥ $107,432 including ≥ $684/week salary; perform at least one exempt duty

State overtime laws: stricter than federal

States may enact overtime rules more favorable to employees than the FLSA. Notable examples:

  • California: Daily overtime at 1.5× after 8 hours in a workday; double-time after 12 hours; seventh consecutive workday triggers OT after 8 hours
  • Colorado: Daily overtime at 1.5× after 12 hours in a workday or 12 consecutive hours regardless of workday
  • Alaska, Nevada, Puerto Rico: Also have daily overtime provisions
