How to Fill Out Your W-4 in 2026: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

The W-4 form (Employee's Withholding Certificate) tells your employer how much federal income tax to withhold from each paycheck. Getting it right means you won't face a surprise tax bill or over-donate an interest-free loan to the IRS.

By FreePayrollCalc Editorial • Updated September 2026 • 8 min read

What is the W-4 and when do you need to update it?

The W-4 is used by your employer's payroll department to calculate how much federal income tax to withhold from each paycheck. You must complete one when you start a new job. You should update it whenever:

  • You get married or divorced
  • You have a child (additional dependent credits)
  • You take on a second job or your spouse starts working
  • Your income changes significantly
  • You received a large refund or owed a large amount at filing

The 2020 redesigned W-4: Five steps

The current W-4 was redesigned in 2020 to eliminate allowances. The five steps are:

Step 1 — Personal Information (required): Name, address, SSN, and filing status. Single, Married Filing Jointly, or Qualifying Surviving Spouse.
Step 2 — Multiple Jobs or Spouse Works (if applicable): Check box (c) if you have two jobs simultaneously or are MFJ with a working spouse. This prevents under-withholding from combined income. Alternatively, use the IRS online estimator.
Step 3 — Claim Dependents (reduces withholding): $2,000 per qualifying child under 17 + $500 per other dependent. Enter the total dollar amount. This directly reduces your annual withholding by the amount entered.
Step 4 — Other Adjustments (optional): (a) Other income not from jobs (investments, rental income) — add it here so tax is withheld. (b) Deductions — if you plan to itemize above $15,000 (single) or $30,000 (MFJ), enter the excess. (c) Extra withholding — enter a flat additional amount per period if needed.
Step 5 — Sign and Date (required): Your signature certifies accuracy under penalty of perjury.

Common W-4 mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Not updating after life changes: Getting married saves significant taxes but only if you update your W-4 to MFJ status. Your employer withholds at single rates until you update it.
  • Multiple jobs — both jobs withholding at single rates: Each employer withholds assuming that job is your only income. Two jobs can push you into a higher bracket. Always check box 2(c) or use the IRS estimator.
  • Claiming too many dependents: If your income is too high for the child tax credit (phases out above $200,000 single / $400,000 MFJ), don't claim them on Step 3 — you won't qualify and will under-withhold.

How to check if your withholding is correct

Use our Tax Withholding Calculator to estimate your annual withholding vs. your estimated actual tax liability. If the gap is large in either direction, submit a new W-4. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator at irs.gov/W4app is the most accurate tool for complex situations (multiple jobs, itemized deductions, other income).
