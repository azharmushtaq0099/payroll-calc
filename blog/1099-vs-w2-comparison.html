1099 vs W-2 in 2026: Which Pays More After Taxes?

The debate between 1099 contractor and W-2 employee status comes down to more than just the gross pay number. Once you factor in self-employment tax, benefits, and employer contributions, the true cost comparison often surprises both workers and employers.

By FreePayrollCalc Editorial • Updated September 2026 • 8 min read

The core difference: who pays FICA?

As a W-2 employee, your employer pays half of FICA (7.65%) and you pay the other half (7.65%) through withholding. As a 1099 contractor, you pay both halves — the full 15.3% self-employment tax on net earnings (technically 92.35% of net earnings × 15.3% = 14.13% effective).

This means a $60,000 1099 contract and a $60,000 W-2 salary are NOT equivalent — the contractor earns less after all taxes.

The 1099/W-2 equivalency formula

To find the equivalent 1099 rate for a W-2 salary, you need to account for:

  1. The 7.65% employer FICA contribution (you now pay it)
  2. The value of employer-paid benefits (health insurance, 401k match, PTO)
  3. The deduction for half of SE tax (reduces income tax)
Quick conversion formula:
Equivalent 1099 rate = W-2 salary ÷ (1 − 0.0765) + benefits value
= $60,000 ÷ 0.9235 + $12,000 benefits ≈ $76,972 to break even

When 1099 makes financial sense

  • Your hourly or project rate is 40–50% higher than W-2 equivalent
  • You have significant deductible business expenses (home office, equipment, vehicle)
  • You prefer flexibility and multiple clients over job security
  • You've structured through an S-Corp to minimize SE tax
  • You have your own affordable health insurance options
