What is cost per hire and why does it matter?

Cost per hire (CPH) is one of the most widely used recruiting metrics, defined by SHRM and ANSI as the total sum of internal and external recruiting costs divided by the total number of hires in a given period. Tracking CPH helps HR leaders benchmark their recruiting efficiency, justify hiring budgets, and identify where spending is too high.

The SHRM formula: Cost Per Hire = (Internal Recruiting Costs + External Recruiting Costs) ÷ Total Hires. Internal costs include HR and recruiter time, hiring manager interview hours, and employee referral bonuses paid. External costs include job board fees, staffing agency fees, background checks, drug tests, and pre-employment assessments.

SHRM 2024 benchmark: The average cost per hire across all US organizations is approximately $4,700. Organizations with fewer than 500 employees average $3,900 per hire. Executive and senior leadership roles can exceed $28,000 per hire when agency fees and extended search timelines are factored in.

Many organizations undercount their true CPH because they track external vendor invoices but not internal staff time. For HR budgeting and board reporting, this calculator includes a fully-loaded view — recruiter and interviewer time converted to dollars, referral bonuses, all vendor costs, and a first-week onboarding figure. FAQ

Cost per hire questions answered

According to SHRM's most recent benchmark data, the average cost per hire in the US is approximately $4,700. For companies with fewer than 500 employees, the average is lower at around $3,900 per hire. These figures vary significantly by industry, role level, and hiring method — executive and technical roles routinely exceed $10,000–$20,000 per hire when agency fees and lost productivity are included. Healthcare, engineering, and legal roles typically have the highest cost per hire due to specialized sourcing and longer time-to-fill.
The SHRM/ANSI cost-per-hire formula is: Cost Per Hire = (Total Internal Recruiting Costs + Total External Recruiting Costs) ÷ Total Hires in Period.

Internal costs include: recruiter and HR staff time (salary + benefits), hiring manager and interviewer time, and employee referral bonuses paid.

External costs include: job board fees (LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter), staffing agency or headhunter fees, background checks, drug testing, pre-employment assessments, and any outsourced recruiting vendor fees.

Many organizations add onboarding costs for a fully-loaded view: new hire training time, equipment setup, and onboarding software or materials.
The most effective strategies for reducing cost per hire:

1. Employee referral programs — Referred candidates typically cost 40–60% less to hire than job board candidates and have higher retention rates.

2. Improve job descriptions — Better descriptions reduce unqualified applications, cutting screening and interview time significantly.

3. Build a talent pipeline — Maintaining a candidate pipeline reduces dependency on paid job boards and eliminates agency fees for predictable roles.

4. Reduce time-to-hire — Every additional week of open role extends interviewer hours and lost-productivity costs. Streamlined interview processes pay dividends across every hire.

5. Use free channels first — LinkedIn's free features, company career pages, and Indeed's organic listings can fill many roles before paid promotion is needed.
