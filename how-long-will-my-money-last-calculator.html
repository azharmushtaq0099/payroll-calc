The 4% Rule Explained

The 4% rule comes from the Trinity Study (1998), which found that a portfolio of 50-75% stocks could sustain a 4% initial withdrawal rate (inflation-adjusted) for 30 years with over 95% success historically. For a $500,000 portfolio, this means $20,000/year or about $1,667/month.

What Can Make Your Money Last Longer

  • Delay Social Security to age 70 — replaces $1,000-$2,000+/month of withdrawals
  • Part-time income even for 5-7 years cuts the portfolio draw dramatically
  • Flexible spending — reduce withdrawals in down-market years
  • Bond/cash buffer — keep 1-2 years of expenses in cash to avoid selling stocks in downturns
