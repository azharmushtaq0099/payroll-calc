Best CD Strategy in 2026

With rates still elevated, locking in a 1-2 year CD at 4.5-5%+ makes sense for money you won't need soon. The key risk: if rates rise after you lock in, you're stuck at the lower rate (or pay an early withdrawal penalty to exit).

CD Laddering — Beat the Rate Risk

Instead of putting everything in one CD, split it across 5 CDs: 1-year, 2-year, 3-year, 4-year, and 5-year. Each year, as one matures, you reinvest at the current best rate. You always have access to 20% of your money every 12 months, and you average out the interest rate cycle.

CD vs High-Yield Savings Account

HYSAs offer flexibility (withdraw anytime) but rates are variable. CDs lock your rate for the term. If you're confident you won't need the money and rates are high, a CD wins. If you might need funds, HYSA gives you optionality.
