How Federal Tax Withholding Works

The US tax system is "pay as you go" — you pay tax throughout the year either through employer withholding from paychecks (W-2 employees) or quarterly estimated payments (freelancers, business owners, investors). Fail to pay enough during the year and you owe an underpayment penalty when you file.

W-4 Withholding: For Employees

W-2 employees control their federal withholding via IRS Form W-4. The redesigned 2020 W-4 uses five steps — most employees only complete Steps 1 and 5. The optional steps adjust for:

  • Step 2: Multiple jobs or a working spouse — prevents significant underwithholding
  • Step 3: Child tax credits and dependent credits — reduces withholding
  • Step 4a: Other non-wage income not subject to withholding (interest, dividends)
  • Step 4b: Additional deductions (itemized, student loan interest, IRA contributions)
  • Step 4c: Extra flat dollar amount withheld per pay period

Use the W-4 withholding calculator to get exact dollar amounts for Steps 3 and 4c based on your actual income and filing situation.

Quarterly Estimated Taxes: For the Self-Employed

If you earn income without withholding, you generally must make quarterly estimated tax payments. The 2026 due dates are:

  • Q1 (Jan–Mar income): April 15, 2026
  • Q2 (Apr–May income): June 16, 2026
  • Q3 (Jun–Aug income): September 15, 2026
  • Q4 (Sep–Dec income): January 15, 2027

The Safe Harbor Rule

You avoid the underpayment penalty if you pay at least (a) 90% of the current year's tax liability, or (b) 100% of the prior year's tax (110% if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000). Most freelancers use the prior-year safe harbor as a baseline, then adjust upward when income grows.

1099 Workers: What Higher Taxes Actually Mean

Independent contractors pay self-employment tax at 15.3% on the first $176,100 of net SE income — covering both the employee and employer FICA share. However, half of SE tax is deductible, business expenses reduce taxable income significantly, and there is no employer claiming your labor for below-market rates. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I am withholding enough federal tax?

Your withholding is sufficient if your estimated annual withholding will equal at least 90% of your current year tax liability, or 100% of last year's total tax (110% if last year's AGI exceeded $150,000). Use the W-4 withholding calculator to check and adjust.

What happens if too little federal tax is withheld?

If you underpay by more than $1,000 and fall below the safe harbor thresholds, the IRS charges an underpayment penalty — currently calculated at the federal short-term rate plus 3% (approximately 8% annualized). File IRS Form 2210 to calculate or waive the penalty.

When are 2026 quarterly estimated taxes due?

Q1 is due April 15, 2026; Q2 is due June 16, 2026; Q3 is due September 15, 2026; Q4 is due January 15, 2027. If a deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday, it shifts to the next business day.

Should I claim 0 or 1 on my W-4?

The redesigned 2020 W-4 no longer uses allowances — there is no "0 or 1" to claim. Completing only Steps 1 and 5 gives you standard withholding for a single-job filer. Add extra withholding in Step 4c if you have side income, or use Step 3 to reduce withholding for child tax credits.

What is the difference between a tax deduction and a tax credit?

A deduction reduces your taxable income — its value depends on your marginal bracket (a $1,000 deduction saves $220 in the 22% bracket). A tax credit directly reduces your tax bill dollar-for-dollar regardless of bracket — a $1,000 credit always saves exactly $1,000.
