529 Plan Tips to Maximize Your Savings

Start as early as possible. For a newborn, 18 years of compounding does most of the work — $300/month for 18 years at 7% is $117,000, but the same $300/month started at age 8 only grows to $50,000.

Use age-based portfolios. Most 529 plans offer age-based options that automatically shift from aggressive (stocks) to conservative (bonds/cash) as college approaches. This prevents a market downturn in the year before college from devastating your balance.

Check your state's tax deduction. Over 30 states offer a state income tax deduction or credit for 529 contributions. In a state with a 5% income tax and a $10,000 deduction cap, contributing $10,000/year saves $500 upfront — a guaranteed 5% return before investment gains.

Grandparent contributions. Since 2024, grandparent-owned 529s no longer impact financial aid (FAFSA change). Grandparents can superfund ($90,000 one-time) without gift tax. This is one of the most efficient wealth transfer strategies available.
