Quick Answer

Your hourly take-home pay is your gross hourly wages minus federal income tax (2026 IRS brackets), Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and state income tax. For a $20/hour worker in Texas at 40 hours/week, take-home is approximately $14.80–$15.40/hour after all federal taxes.

How hourly paycheck taxes work in 2026

When you earn an hourly wage, your employer withholds federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and state income tax from each paycheck. Understanding each component helps you verify your pay stub and plan your budget accurately.

Gross pay = (regular hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × 1.5). Under the FLSA, overtime is required for non-exempt employees working over 40 hours per week. Some states — California, Colorado, Alaska — require daily overtime for hours over 8 in a workday.

Federal income tax is calculated by annualizing your per-period gross wages, subtracting the 2026 standard deduction ($15,000 single, $30,000 married), then applying marginal brackets from 10% to 37%. The result is divided back to a per-paycheck withholding amount.

FAQ

Hourly Paycheck Calculator questions answered

Multiply your hourly rate by hours worked to get gross pay, then add overtime hours × rate × 1.5. From that gross amount subtract: Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), federal income tax using 2026 IRS Publication 15-T brackets, and your state income tax. The remainder is your net take-home pay.
Overtime wages are taxed at the same marginal rates — there is no special overtime tax rate. However, overtime pushes your annualized income higher, which may move more of your income into a higher federal bracket. The effective tax increase on overtime is usually small for most workers.
FICA takes 7.65% total: Social Security at 6.2% (on wages up to $176,100 in 2026) plus Medicare at 1.45% on all wages. Your employer matches this amount separately. High earners above $200,000 ($250,000 married) pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surcharge.
Nine states have no individual income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Workers in these states only pay federal income tax and FICA, keeping significantly more per paycheck than workers in high-tax states like California (up to 13.3%) or New York (up to 10.9%).
