How Much Does COBRA Cost in 2026?

Most employees are shocked by COBRA costs because they've only ever seen their paycheck deduction — not the employer's contribution. COBRA requires you to pay 102% of the full plan cost, including the portion your employer was paying.

Average COBRA Costs in 2026

Based on the KFF 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, average annual premiums for employer-sponsored health insurance in 2026 are approximately $8,951 for employee-only coverage and $25,572 for family coverage. Under COBRA, you pay 102% of those totals — meaning:

  • Employee-only COBRA: approximately $760/month
  • Employee + spouse: approximately $1,500–$1,800/month
  • Family COBRA: approximately $2,200/month

These are national averages. Your actual COBRA cost depends on the specific plan your employer offers, their carrier, and your location. Use the calculator above with your actual plan premium for an exact figure.

Why COBRA Is So Expensive

If your employer covered 83% of your health plan premium, your $150/month paycheck deduction was just 17% of an $882/month plan. On COBRA, you pay $882 × 102% = $899/month — a roughly 6× increase for identical coverage. The employer contribution simply disappears, and you absorb the full cost plus the 2% administrative fee allowed by law.

How to Calculate Your COBRA Premium

  1. Find your full plan premium — check your benefits enrollment materials or ask HR for the "employer + employee" combined premium. This is different from your paycheck deduction.
  2. Add 2% — multiply the full premium by 1.02 for the administrative fee
  3. That's your monthly COBRA cost — for example: $900 full premium × 1.02 = $918/month COBRA

When COBRA Makes Financial Sense

  • You have already met your annual deductible and need ongoing care — switching plans resets your deductible
  • You or a dependent needs continuity of care with specific in-network providers
  • Your new job starts within 1–3 months and the coverage gap would be very short
  • Your income is above ~$60,000 and ACA subsidies are minimal
  • You are between plans mid-year and need specific medication that isn't covered on marketplace plans

COBRA vs ACA Marketplace: Cost Comparison

For most people who lose job-based coverage, the ACA marketplace is significantly cheaper than COBRA — especially with premium tax credits. A 35-year-old earning $45,000/year in 2026 pays approximately $200–$350/month for a Silver plan with subsidies, compared to $760/month for employee-only COBRA. Job loss is a qualifying life event that triggers a 60-day special enrollment window on the marketplace — you don't have to wait for open enrollment.

Exception: If you've already met your deductible for the year, the short-term math may favor COBRA even at the higher premium. Run the numbers before deciding.

FAQ

COBRA insurance questions answered

How much does COBRA cost in 2026?
COBRA costs 102% of the full plan premium — employer share + employee share + 2% admin fee. Average 2026 costs: ~$750/month for employee-only plans, ~$2,200/month for family coverage (KFF 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey).
How long does COBRA last?
18 months for job loss or reduction in hours; 36 months for divorce, death of covered employee, or a dependent aging off; up to 29 months with a disability extension. Coverage ends early if you fail to pay premiums or enroll in other qualifying coverage.
Can I cancel COBRA at any time?
Yes. You can drop COBRA whenever you get other coverage (new job, marriage, Medicare, Medicaid, etc.). You are not locked in for the full 18 months. However, you cannot re-enroll once you've dropped it.
Is COBRA retroactive?
Yes. You have 60 days to elect COBRA after your qualifying event. If you elect it later in that window, coverage is retroactive to the loss date — meaning you can wait and only elect if you actually need care. You'd owe back premiums for the whole period.
